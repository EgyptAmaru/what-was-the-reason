What Was the Reason?

A social reasoning game. Think out loud together.

How to play

A social reasoning game. The theme is math, but every question is really about thinking clearly. The goal is to get a group thinking out loud together and make being wrong feel safe. Expect your gut to be wrong often.

Who plays

Teams and host. Two teams and one host. Teams of 3 to 4 work best, but any size plays the same. The host runs the board, keeps time, and judges answers.

Calculators. Allowed for arithmetic only.

The board

Layout. Four columns, six rows. Columns are ways of reasoning; rows are difficulty, worth more points further down. A square shows only its points until it is opened.

Three acts. Rows unlock two at a time: clear both rows of an act to open the next.

  • Act 1 · Rows 1–2 · Solo. Players answer alone, with generous time. Teams alternate questions.
  • Act 2 · Rows 3–4 · Team discussion. The picking team talks it out; the other team stays quiet.
  • Act 3 · Rows 5–6 · Simultaneous. Both teams work the same question in one shared window, commit an answer with reasoning, and reveal together.

Taking turns. Picks alternate between teams and rotate through teammates; the picker need not answer. In acts 2 and 3, a rotating spokesperson gives the team's final answer. A wrong answer belongs to the team, not the speaker.

Wrong answers. Never lose points. In acts 1 and 2 a miss offers the question to the other team as an optional steal (in act 2 they may use what they overheard). If nobody gets it, the host reveals the answer and retires the question.

Scoring. An answer meets the question's criteria or it does not; sound reasoning can win without finishing the math. In act 3 both teams score if both reason well, and showing the math beats reasoning alone.

The host

Running the game. The host judges answers against each question's criteria (no single exact answer needed), keeps the room encouraging, and frames hard questions so a wrong guess feels expected.

Host console (optional). On a second device, the host can see hints, notes, and answers for the open question and control the board (open, flip, pick winners, timer). It is optional; the game runs fully from the main screen. To pair, tap "Host console" and scan the code.
